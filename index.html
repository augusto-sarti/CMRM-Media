Auditory scene analysis

The following demonstrations concern perceptual grouping: the same physical event sequence may be organized into different auditory streams or objects.

Sequential stream segregation — Bregman-style reconstruction

A repeating six-tone high/low cycle is heard first slowly and then rapidly. At the faster rate, listen for its progressive reorganization into separate high and low streams.

Grouping by timbral similarity

Two interleaved ostinati are heard first with a shared timbre and then with contrasting timbres. Compare how easily the latent lines can be followed in the two conditions.

Interleaved familiar melodies

Two familiar melodies are interleaved while their register separation changes. Listen for the transition from a densely interwoven sequence to two more readily traceable melodic streams.

Perceptual restoration of interrupted speech

An utterance is interrupted first by silence and then by masking noise at the same locations. Compare how strongly the surviving speech fragments are perceptually connected across the interruptions.

Chapter 4 — Tonal Organization and Pitch Space

Video demonstrations accompanying the discussion of relative pitch coordinates, modal representations, and pitch-space geometry.

Scale-relative coordinates reveal periodicity

The same melodic pattern can look irregular in chromatic semitone coordinates and regular when motion is measured in steps of the active scale.

Melodic pattern in semitone and modal-step coordinates

In the upper display, successive-note distances are measured in semitones; the resulting sequence does not expose a simple periodic pattern. In the lower display, the same melodic motion is expressed as scale-step displacements relative to the active mode, revealing the repeated structure. This is the representation change discussed in the chapter as a separation of melodic pattern from modal context.
